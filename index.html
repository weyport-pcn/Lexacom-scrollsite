3Clinical safety and data protection

Before Lexacom is used with patients, including in a pilot, the national standard expects a signed DCB0160 and a completed DPIA.

Each practice's CSO decides when that point is reached, and can sign a first version with conditions for a pilot, then update it as the practice learns.

Two standards apply. Lexacom, as the manufacturer, holds the DCB0129 safety case and hazard log for the product. Each practice, as the organisation deploying it, completes a DCB0160 covering how it is used safely there, signed by the practice CSO.

Rather than five practices writing five safety cases from scratch, we are building a shared PCN core. With their agreement, it draws on Weymouth Bay's safety case and Royal Manor's draft DCB0160, which sets out a local list of 24 hazards, checked against Lexacom's DCB0129 and hazard log. Each practice adopts it, adds local detail, and its own CSO signs it off. This keeps the work consistent, reduces the load on CSOs, and leaves accountability where the standard puts it.

What Lexacom's documents mean for practices

  • Documents received: Clinical Safety Case Report v2.0, Clinical Risk Management Plan v2.0, Hazard Log v3.0 and DPIA v1.7. Lexacom will reissue them with its 26.4 release in December, so practice sign-offs should name the versions used and be reviewed when 26.4 arrives.
  • Shared risks: Lexacom's hazard log lists 18 hazards and marks 13 of them as shared with the deploying practice, mainly relying on clinician review of every note and on training. The local controls in each practice's DCB0160 are what manage these.
  • DPIA: Lexacom's DPIA is written from its side as the data processor. Each practice completes its own as data controller. For direct care, NHS England's guidance points to Article 6(1)(e) public task and Article 9(2)(h) health and care as the most likely lawful bases.
  • NHS England templates: a template DPIA for ambient scribes, and a template data sharing and processing agreement, on the NHS England IG templates page.

4How we chose Lexacom

Over 18 months we evaluated ambient voice technology suppliers against the NHS AVT Supplier Registry, with Health Innovation Wessex involved from the start. Mark shortlisted four: Lexacom, TORTUS (via X-on Health), Tandem (via Accurx) and Heidi Health. The PCN Core and Federation meeting then selected Lexacom as the supplier to proceed with, and we published the evaluation on the national AVID forum.

All five practices have seen at least one demo, and we have seen Lexacom's current release (version 26.3). Version 26.4 is due in December.

6PCN services

Lexacom has confirmed PCN staff are covered when delivering services for participating practices. Our care coordinators and frailty team have long, complex phone calls with patients, families and carers, so they could benefit most.

Lexacom's capture audio feature records the computer's sound at the press of a button, so it works with our 3CX phones without integration. Because nothing is integrated, Lexacom does not record which phone systems its customers use, so it cannot point us to another PCN using it with 3CX.

Instead, Lexacom has offered a trial for these teams, with ambient templates built for their roles, and a demo for any staff who missed the earlier ones. Lexacom's own experience outside traditional clinical settings is that results are best when the service defines the outputs it needs before any pilot. That matches our plan: we will first agree with the teams what a good output looks like, and settle whose safety case covers staff working across several practices, before any trial.
